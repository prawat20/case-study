# Caselet 2 — Stage A: Personas + Problems

> Working draft for §1 of the final submission. Two personas, one section. Each persona owns its own problem list. Brief asks "Persona" singular + "list of Problems" plural — two persona blocks each with their own problems reads natural AND reinforces Plottwyst's core platform thesis: one engine, two surfaces, two demand vectors.

---

## Persona A — The Multiplayer Host

**Who they are.** The person in the group who organizes the thing. Two flavors of the same archetype:

- **Friend-group host** — the one with the recurring Saturday-night Zoom, the bachelorette party planner, the cousin who runs family game night. Owns the calendar invite. Picks the game. Feels the social cost when it falls flat.
- **Corporate event host** — manager, People Ops lead, EA tasked with the offsite or the quarterly virtual team-building session. Has a budget line and a "did the team enjoy it?" survey to answer for. Same person at a different scale.

Both share one job-to-be-done: *give a group of 4–12 people a memorable shared session that isn't a repeat of the last one.*

**The Problems they face.**

1. **Game fatigue is fast.** Werewolf, Mafia, Among Us, One Night Ultimate all collapse after 2–3 sessions with the same group. Once everyone has seen the bluffs, the surprise dies. Among Us DAU dropped 95% from peak for exactly this reason — same maps, same game.
2. **"More content" is the only fix offered.** JackBox sells new packs every year; after 5 plays of Trivia Murder Party the answers repeat. The content-pipeline model can't outrun a motivated friend group.
3. **Premium experiences don't scale.** $200/seat murder-mystery dinners ship the same hand-authored script every night. Escape rooms work once per group per year. Both require physical co-location, which kills remote/hybrid use entirely.
4. **Setup tax kills spontaneity.** Board games need physical kit + 20 min of rules + a moderator who's read the manual. The host pays the cost; the group pays in dead time before the fun starts.
5. **No personalisation.** Off-the-shelf games don't bend to the specific group — the new-hire welcome, the bachelorette, the bereaved family wanting something lighter than Cards Against Humanity. The host either picks a generic option or builds something custom (which nobody has time to do).
6. **Corporate hosts carry an extra burden: outcomes.** They have to justify the spend with engagement, retention, or culture impact — and existing options are either too gimmicky (escape-the-room-style icebreakers) or too undifferentiated to defend in a budget review.

## Persona B — The Daily Puzzle Player

**Who they are.** The Wordle-shaped player. ~5 minutes a day, often coffee-adjacent. Ritual matters as much as the puzzle. Shares results with one or two group chats. Tracks a streak even when nobody else is looking. No learning curve tolerated. The puzzle is finishable in one sitting or it doesn't exist.

This persona owns commute-time, lunch-break, and "between meetings" minutes — the slots Wordle proved were monetizable for the NYT at $1B/year combined puzzle revenue.

**The Problems they face.**

1. **Genre fatigue under the surface.** Wordle, Connections, Strands, Mini Crossword, Spelling Bee — all letter/grid puzzles with the same underlying constraint. Once a player has solved 200 Wordles, the starter word is optimised and variance drops. The genre exhausted its design space three years in.
2. **No narrative payoff.** The daily ritual gives 5 minutes of *"I solved something"* — but no story, no character, no consequence. Just letters. The dopamine plateau is real and well-documented in the Connections subreddit.
3. **Spoiler-hostile sharing.** Wordle's coloured-square share solved this for *outcome* sharing — but you can't share *what happened* without spoiling tomorrow's puzzle for someone in a different timezone. Narrative content has the same problem at 10×.
4. **Streak addiction without depth.** The streak counter is the only retention mechanic; the game underneath has not meaningfully changed since 2021. Players know it. The replay value is pure ritual, not surprise.
5. **Single-mode trap.** The morning daily ritual is a walled garden. It doesn't extend into evening play, group play, or any longer-form experience for the same player when they want more. Players who want depth have to switch products entirely — and most don't bother.

## Why two personas, not one

The two personas share an underlying tension and split on session shape:

- **Multiplayer Host** needs *bounded social variance* — a new shared experience per session, group-sized, 30–60 min, with a memorable outcome.
- **Daily Player** needs *bounded solo variance* — a new individual experience per day, single-player, 5–10 min, with a shareable badge.

Both unmet needs trace to the same supply-side gap: **content scaled, gameplay systems didn't**. Hand-authored games (Mystery dinners, JackBox packs, escape rooms) and grid puzzles (Wordle and descendants) both hit a content ceiling that no amount of writing or design-grind has solved.

This is why Plottwyst is structured as one engine and two surfaces, not one product. The same generation+validation system serves both demand vectors. The platform thesis — and the rest of this pitch — falls out of that choice.
